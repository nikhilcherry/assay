# Innovation Claim: Evidence That Knows Its Own Weight

Which detector fired on which exam case comes from `docs/r2/FACTS.md`, generated by
`python -m assay.r2.factsheet` from a live run of the shipped agent. What each component changes is
in `docs/r2/ABLATION.md` (`python -m assay.r2.ablation`). Whether each claim reproduces is in
`docs/r2/VERIFY_CLAIMS.md` (`python -m assay.r2.verify_claims`).

## 1. The idea
Half the exam is legitimate, and blocking or reporting a legitimate customer scores zero. So in
Assay no signal gets a vote until it has been measured, and every signal gets exactly the vote its
measurement earns:

- **Measured weight.** A detector joins `ENABLED` (`assay/r2/investigate_r2.py`) with a likelihood
  ratio backtested as-of (`t <= opened_at`) against the bank's labelled closed cases. Where the sample
  is small we use the 95% CI lower bound.
- **Link, not vote.** A pattern that never happened before January has no measurable LR. It still
  ties the case to other cards (policy §3a), but it weighs `lr = 1.0`. We never turn rarity into a
  weight.
- **Escalate, never decide.** Untrusted text and conflicting records can only send a case to a person.
  They never add weight toward fraud and never clear.

Every finding carries a re-runnable attestation (query, parameters, exact ids, dataset hash). We
re-run all 177 of them ourselves (`verify_claims`): 0 mismatches, 0 unresolvable.

## 3. Where it changes the outcome (ablation, `docs/r2/ABLATION.md`)
- **Without `cpc`**, HHF-001 and HHF-002 go from fraud to legitimate ($4,355.73 no longer acted on).
- **Without `micro_drip_cohort`**, HHF-004 loses its 16 linked cards and its report. A $23.99 loss that
  connects to other cards needs a report under §3a.
- **Without `ring_wcc`**, HHF-005 drops from 0.96 to 0.76 and loses the 11 ring cards. It keeps its
  report through the micro-drip cohort.
- **Without the card-mismatch guard**, HHF-011 is cleared as legitimate on an unestablished cardholder.
- **Without the record-conflict guard**, HHF-008 is closed as legitimate, although the alert says
  "online" and the bank records the charge in person.
- **Without `vector_precedent`**, HHF-013 closes without asking the customer. With it, a $409.30 charge
  whose 20 nearest prior cases were all fraud gets an R1 verification first.
- **Without the measured dispute weight**, HHF-004 drops from fraud to uncertain.
- `burst_hub` and `campaign` change no exam case. They are kept for what they caught in history and
  reported as such.

## 4. Measured negatives (switched off because the numbers said so)
- **New-device / account-takeover detectors** (`docs/r2/C4_new_device.md`): against cleared closed
  cases, neither `new_device_burst` nor `device_churn` passes the gate (CI low < 1.5). `card_id` is an
  issuer bucket, not a person. So HHF-009 (a new LG-H831 device, anonymous.com, 4.4x the median) is
  verified and closed, not escalated. Measured against random card-days, the same detector "scores"
  LR 51. We did not use that number, because it double-counts the alert selection.
- **Unmeasured fan-in weight**: the old assumed LR 3.0 is gone.
- **Per-card micro-charges** (LR 0.63), **"innocence" signals**, and an **uncapped episode builder** all lost
  to simpler baselines (`docs/r2/B6_micro_fanin.md`, `B29_innocence.md`, `N7_dispute_episode.md`).

## 5. Guards that change outcomes
- **January drift** (`docs/r2/B1_model.md`): only 1.0670% of January rows are in-distribution, so a
  January score can raise suspicion but never clear a case.
- **Card mismatch** (DQ-07): HHF-011 names C09241-K1, but the bank's own 21 closed cases name this
  debit card K2. The case is uncertain and escalated.
- **Record conflict** (R8): HHF-008's alert says "online" and the bank's record says in person.
- **Ring caps**: elements on more than 15 cards are dropped. HHF-014/016 sit in a 121-card component
  that is correctly not called a ring.

## 6. Tested by adversaries
Ten Argon attacker personas wrote 80 blind scenarios (`docs/r2/REDTEAM.md`). After that, eight Argon
devil's advocates argued three rounds over which features to build (innovation, drills, answer key,
policy, TigerGraph, engineering, demo, rival). They forced the cohort links, the removal of the
assumed fan-in LR, the claim re-runner and the ablation. We overruled them where the numbers did:
no new-device vote, and no forcing uncertain probabilities into a "calibrated-looking" band.

**Weakest point, stated plainly.** For an uncertain case resting on a conflict (HHF-008, HHF-011), the
probability stays the model's 0.01, because the conflict concerns whose card it is or what was
charged, not how risky the charge is. We escalate rather than invent a number.
